JevIt Help

JevIt sorts your mail with recipes. A recipe is one plain-language yes/no question, such as “Is this an invoice or receipt?”. TypeSafe's Jev model answers it for each email with a probability. When the probability reaches the recipe's threshold, JevIt adds the recipe's tag, and can also star the email, mark it read, mark it as junk, or move it to a folder.

Getting started

  1. Open the manager with the JevIt toolbar button (except while triage runs: then a click stops the run). It also opens by itself after installing.
  2. Pick the Service that runs Jev: TypeSafe (the default), OpenRouter, or Open-Jev on this computer.
  3. Read who receives your mail and tick “JevIt may send the content of emails I triage to …”. Nothing is sent before you do.
  4. Paste your API key for that service (from typesafe.ai or openrouter.ai/keys; Open-Jev only needs one if your server asks). It is saved when you leave the field and never shown again. Paste a new one to replace it, or click Remove key.
  5. Optional: tick Automatically run recipes on new mail, then click Save.
  6. Try it: open an email and click JevIt in the message header to see what Jev thinks.

Five recipes are installed to start with: Spam, Needs reply, Newsletter, Invoice / receipt and Urgent. By default they only add a tag.

Services. TypeSafe runs Jev itself. OpenRouter passes your mail on to the model's provider: TypeSafe for jev-latest or typesafe/jev-1.13, or another company for a model like openai/…, and the consent text says so. Open-Jev runs on your own computer, at 127.0.0.1 and the port you set. The service, key, model and consent box save as soon as you change them. Changing the service, or picking a model from another provider, unticks consent, so nothing goes to a new receiver until you allow it. Each service keeps its own key, and its own stored scores.

Triaging mail

Automatically
With “Automatically run recipes on new mail” on, every new email in your inboxes and normal folders is triaged as it arrives. Mail that moves away while JevIt works on it, such as to Junk by Thunderbird's junk filter a few seconds after it arrives, is left alone; that isn't an error.
Selected mail
Select one or more emails, right-click and choose JevIt → Triage with Jev. To triage a whole folder, press Ctrl+A first. Emails are handled four at a time, so large selections still take a while: the JevIt toolbar button counts the emails done, and clicking it stops the run, including the emails on their way to Jev at that moment: they're left as they were. When it's done, one notification sums up what each recipe matched, how many emails are Unsure, and anything skipped or failed.
One email, with review
Open an email and click JevIt in the message header. The popup shows Jev's probability for every recipe and ticks the ones that reach their threshold. Change the ticks if you disagree and click Apply. With “Remember my corrections as examples” on, every tick you changed becomes a learned example.

Triaging an email again replaces JevIt's tags on it. Your own Thunderbird tags are never touched. Moves, stars, read and junk flags are not undone.

Skip already triaged mail (Connection section, on by default): when you triage selected mail, emails Jev already judged (they have the Triaged tag, or stored scores from the last 90 days) are skipped, so triaging a whole folder again doesn't cost anything twice. Untick it to apply your recipes to them again, for example after changing a threshold. An email whose triage failed isn't skipped, for example when moving it failed or Jev's answer had no scores in it: triaging it again finishes the job, for free if Jev's scores were stored.

Jev's scores are remembered. JevIt stores Jev's score for every email and recipe on your computer, for 90 days. Opening the header popup, or triaging an email again, reuses them for free, even when this month's budget is used up. Only a recipe you edited or taught since, or a new recipe, is sent to Jev again, and so is an email whose content changed. Forget stored scores (Usage & budget section) deletes them all, so Jev is asked again; mail with the Triaged tag is still skipped unless you untick Skip already triaged mail.

Never send mail in account … to Jev (Connection section, one per account): mail in a ticked account is never sent to Jev, whether by automatic triage, selected-mail triage or the header popup. Most settings in the manager show a short explanation when you hover over them.

Recipes

Recipes are listed in the manager under Recipes. Each one shows as a single line: its colours, name, action, threshold and number of examples. Click it to edit.

Question
The yes/no question Jev answers. Refer to the email as `email` and to yourself as `me`, for example: “Does a person in `email` ask `me` to reply?”
Counts as yes / Counts as no
What a yes and a no mean, with boundary cases. Jev reads these literally, so write down what you actually mean.
Threshold
How sure Jev must be before the recipe matches, from 0 to 1. 0.8 means 80% sure. Choose it by what a wrong answer costs: the more a recipe does to your mail, the surer Jev must be. Good starting points:
  • Tag + star: 0.3–0.5. Better an extra star than a missed request.
  • Only tag: 0.5. A wrong tag costs little either way.
  • Tag + mark read: 0.7–0.8. A wrong match hides a mail you should have seen.
  • Junk or move: 0.9. A real mail moved away is far worse than one spam left in your inbox.
Then check it in the popup on a few mails that should and shouldn't match. If their percentages overlap, no threshold will separate them: sharpen the yes/no descriptions or teach the recipe with “This is / This is not”.
Tag key
The tag's internal name: lowercase letters, digits and _, unique per recipe. If you change it, mail tagged earlier keeps the old tag.

Adding recipes. Use Add recipe… under the list. You can choose a blank recipe or a ready-made one: Meeting / event, Shipping, Security alert, Phishing, Personal, Social or Jobs / recruiting, plus any of the five defaults you removed.

More like this. Select example emails, right-click and choose JevIt → New recipe from selected mail…. This opens a new recipe named after the sender. Its question is “is this the same kind of mail as these examples?”, with the selected emails as yes-examples. Check the wording and click Save. These recipes depend on their examples, so add a few “This is not …” examples when they match too much.

Changes to recipes are kept only after you click Save, either at the top or the bottom of the page.

Actions and folders

A match always adds the recipe's tag. When it matches can do one more thing:

  • Only tag: nothing else.
  • Tag + star, Tag + mark read, or Tag + mark as junk. Mark as junk sets Thunderbird's junk flag.
  • Tag + move to folder, or Tag + mark as junk + move to folder.

Folder choices:

  • A specific folder, such as “work@example.com/Junk”. All matching mail goes there, whatever account it came from.
  • Every account: name: each email moves to the folder with that name in its own account, for example each account's own Junk. If an account doesn't have that folder yet, JevIt creates it at the account's top level the first time it's needed.
  • New folder…: create a folder right from the recipe. Choose where to create it (it starts on your default account), or tick Create in every account to make it in all accounts at once and use it as an “Every account” folder.

An email can only move once. If several moving recipes match, the one Jev is most sure about wins. Moving between accounts works, but is slower because Thunderbird copies the email.

Star, mark read, mark as junk and move only happen when Jev is at least 90% sure (Act only when Jev is this sure in the Connection section). Below that, a matching recipe only sets its tag, see When Jev is unsure.

Teaching Jev

Jev can't be retrained, but you can give each recipe examples. Examples are sent along with the recipe's question, so Jev sees your earlier verdicts.

  • Right-click emails and choose JevIt → recipe → This is … / This is not …. “This is” adds a yes-example, adds the tag and runs the recipe's action. “This is not” adds a no-example and removes the tag.
  • For Spam there are also keyboard shortcuts, see Keyboard shortcuts.
  • In the header popup, corrections become examples when “Remember my corrections as examples” is ticked.

A recipe keeps its 20 newest examples. In the manager, open a recipe and click learned example(s) to see them. Click ✕ to remove one, or Forget examples to clear them all, then Save. Keep examples clear-cut: a wrong example teaches the wrong thing for a long time.

Teaching doesn't ask Jev anything, so it costs nothing.

People you know

A recipe with “Never match mail from people I know” never matches mail from a known sender, whatever Jev says. This is on for Spam by default. There's also a switch for Spam at the top of the manager: “Never tag mail from people I know as Spam”.

A sender counts as known when their exact address is:

  • in one of your local address books. This includes Collected Addresses, where Thunderbird normally keeps everyone you've written to.
  • a recipient of an email in one of your Sent folders.

A From address is easy to fake, so a sender only counts as known when your mail server's DMARC check passed for that sender's domain. JevIt reads this from the Authentication-Results header your mail server adds (Gmail, Outlook and most providers do). Mail that fails DMARC, or has no DMARC result, is judged by Jev like any other mail.

A sender can add a fake Authentication-Results header of their own, so JevIt only trusts the one written by your mail server. It finds that server per account by reading the header on up to 5 recent Inbox mails, inside Thunderbird, and checks again weekly. The manager shows it under “Never tag mail from people I know as Spam”. If it says none found, your mail server doesn't add the header, and no sender counts as known.

Your own addresses never count as known, because spam often fakes them. The check runs inside Thunderbird, and your contacts are never sent anywhere. Online (LDAP) address books aren't searched.

The header popup shows “you know this sender: not tagged” when this applies. You can still tick the recipe by hand.

Thunderbird's junk filter

Simplest: once your Spam recipe has proven itself, turn Thunderbird's own junk filter off and let JevIt do the job, with Spam set to Tag + mark as junk + move to folder. That gives you one source of truth. For each account: Account Settings → Junk Settings → untick Enable adaptive junk mail controls for this account. Thunderbird keeps what it has learned in training.dat in your profile folder. Keep that file in case you switch back.

If you keep Thunderbird's filter on:

  • Untick “Move new junk messages to”. JevIt sees new mail only after Thunderbird's junk check, and only in inboxes and normal folders. Mail that Thunderbird moves to Junk is never triaged, so Thunderbird's mistakes stay hidden there.
  • Expect two verdicts. Thunderbird can mark an email as junk that JevIt doesn't tag as Spam. “This is not Spam” removes JevIt's tag but not Thunderbird's junk flag: click Not Junk for that.

The Triaged tag

Every email Jev has judged gets a Triaged tag, whether or not any recipe matched. Teaching doesn't add it, and neither does a failed triage: when moving the email fails, the tag comes off again. Use Thunderbird's quick filter or a saved search on this tag to see what JevIt has processed, or what it hasn't yet. You can change its name and colour in the Connection section. Rename it there, not in Thunderbird's own tag settings: JevIt sets its tags' names, so a rename there is undone. It starts close to Thunderbird's own text colour, so triaged mail looks as before.

Thunderbird fills a selected row with the colour of its first tag, so a selected triaged email looks different from an untagged one. If that bothers you, untick Tag every judged email “Triaged”. Mail no recipe matched then stays exactly as it was, and “Skip already triaged mail” recognises judged mail by Jev's stored scores instead (for 90 days).

When Jev is unsure

A wrong tag is easy to spot. A wrong move to Junk hides the email. So JevIt is careful when Jev isn't sure:

  • Actions wait for certainty. A recipe that matches with less than 90% certainty only sets its tag. Its star, mark read, junk or move doesn't happen.
  • Near misses are flagged. An email that scored just under a recipe's threshold (within 15 percentage points) gets no recipe tag.

Both kinds of email get the amber Unsure tag. Look through that tag now and then, and give your verdict with JevIt → recipe → This is … / This is not …, or in the header popup. Your verdict runs the recipe's action and removes the Unsure tag. These are the emails Jev learns the most from, so the Unsure tag shrinks as you teach it.

Rename the tag in the Connection section, not in Thunderbird's own tag settings, where JevIt undoes the rename. Change both numbers there too. Act only when Jev is this sure at 0 acts on every match; Unsure margin at 0 turns the Unsure tag off. Applying from the header popup, and teaching, always run the actions you choose.

Tag colours

Thunderbird uses a tag's colour for the email's row text, and has one colour per tag. JevIt therefore gives every recipe two colours: a pale one for dark themes and a deeper one for light themes, in the same hue.

  • Tag colours (Connection section): Follow Thunderbird's theme, or always use the light or dark set. When following the theme, JevIt recolours its tags when the theme changes. If that doesn't happen on your system, pick the set by hand.
  • Each recipe card has both colour boxes (dark · light). Its dot in the list shows both halves.
  • Default colours (next to Add recipe) sets the built-in recipes and Triaged back to the default palette. Recipes you made yourself keep their colours.

Usage and budget

JevIt counts every Jev request, the input tokens Jev reports, and the estimated cost, for this month and for all time. TypeSafe charges only input tokens. Set the price per million tokens if TypeSafe's price changes. OpenRouter reports what each request cost, and JevIt counts that instead.

The monthly budget (default $1, which is roughly 10,000 emails) stops all Jev requests once it's used up, until next month or until you raise it. You'll get a notification, and the JevIt toolbar button shows a $ badge until there's budget again. A triage that is running stops as soon as the budget is used up (also when you lower it below what's spent) or you untick consent. Each request is counted, with an estimate, before it's sent, so emails triaged in parallel can't all start on the last of the budget: at most one request goes past it. Once Jev answers, the estimate is replaced by what Jev reports. A request stopped on its way stays counted with its estimate, as the service may charge it. Mail that arrives meanwhile isn't triaged: it has no Triaged tag, so you can find it and choose JevIt → Triage with Jev later. Set it to 0 for no limit. These are estimates: your bill from TypeSafe or OpenRouter is what counts.

Backup

Export… saves all recipes and their learned examples to a file. Import… loads such a file: recipes with the same tag key are replaced, others are added. Then click Save.

The backup doesn't include your API key, your settings or your usage counters. Folders chosen for moves only work in the same Thunderbird profile. “Every account” folders store just the folder name, so they also work elsewhere.

Keyboard shortcuts

Alt+Shift+SThis is Spam: yes-example, Spam tag and Spam's action, for the selected or open email
Alt+Shift+DThis is not Spam: no-example and remove the Spam tag

To change them: Add-ons and Themes → gear icon → Manage Extension Shortcuts. They work on the recipe with tag key jev_spam.

Privacy

  • Only after you tick consent, and only for mail you triage, JevIt sends the service you picked the following, with your own key: the sender, recipients, subject and text (quoted replies and styling removed, links cut down to their host name and email links to their address, up to 3000 characters; change the text limit in the manager), the Authentication-Results, List-Unsubscribe (its links cut down the same way) and Reply-To headers, the text and host name of up to 10 links (never opened), the names and types of up to 10 attachments (never their contents), your address, and your recipes with their examples.
  • TypeSafe (api.typesafe.ai) and OpenRouter (openrouter.ai) are reached over HTTPS. With OpenRouter, mail goes to OpenRouter and to the provider of the model you select; their policies apply. Open-Jev is reached on this computer (127.0.0.1) and stays there, unless your server sends it on.
  • Nothing is sent to the JevIt author. There's no tracking or analytics.
  • Your API keys, recipes with their examples (sender, subject and a short snippet), Jev's scores, settings and usage counters stay in Thunderbird. The known-sender check runs locally.
  • They are stored in your Thunderbird profile without encryption: anyone with access to the profile can read them. The manager never shows a saved key. Use disk encryption, and an API key with a low spending limit.
  • Untick consent to stop all requests immediately, including those already on their way: their emails are left as they were. JevIt is not affiliated with TypeSafe.

Tips for good recipes

  • One condition per recipe. Two recipes are better than one vague one.
  • Be literal. Jev answers exactly what you wrote. If you find yourself explaining a wrong answer, add that explanation to the yes/no descriptions.
  • Don't ask for dates or maths. “Due within 3 days” is unreliable; “says it's urgent or due very soon” works.
  • Use the popup to tune thresholds. Look at the percentages on a few typical emails and set the threshold between the yes and no cases, towards the safe side for the recipe's action. To apply a new threshold to mail you already triaged, untick “Skip already triaged mail” and triage it again: that's free.
  • Be careful with moves and junk. Start with “Only tag”, and switch to moving once the recipe has proven itself.
  • English works best. Jev handles other languages, but less accurately. Check the first results.
  • Pin the model (for example jev-1.13.0) once your thresholds are tuned, so a new Jev version doesn't change results unexpectedly.

Troubleshooting

A notification says triage failed or can't run
It says why: no API key, consent not given, budget reached, or an error from Jev (for example 401 for an invalid key). Click it to open the manager. For automatic triage you get this at most once per Thunderbird session.
An email has no Triaged tag
It hasn't been triaged, its triage failed, or the Triaged tag is turned off. Select it and choose JevIt → Triage with Jev.
A recipe matches too much or too little
Adjust the threshold, sharpen the yes/no descriptions, or teach it with “This is / This is not”.
Mail from a contact still gets tagged as spam
Check that “Never match mail from people I know” is ticked for the Spam recipe, that the exact sender address is in an address book, that the mail passed DMARC (View → Headers → All shows it in the Authentication-Results header), and that the manager shows a trusted mail server for that account. Some senders don't set up DMARC; teach Jev with “This is not Spam” instead.
